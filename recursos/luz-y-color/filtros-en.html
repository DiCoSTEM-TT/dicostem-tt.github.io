The filter laboratory

Light passes through two filters. What will reach the screen?

Make your prediction

A white source illuminates a white screen. We will place two transparent filters in series: the light passes through one and then the other.

When both filters are placed in the beam, what colour will the illuminated area of the screen appear?

Light is filtered step by step

After checking, you can explore freely. Changing the arrangement does not alter your initial prediction.

What this arrangement represents

In the ideal model, a cyan filter transmits green and blue and absorbs red; magenta transmits red and blue and absorbs green; yellow transmits red and green and absorbs blue. Filters do not add light: they select some of the light they receive.

Surface reflections, additional losses and ambient light are ignored. A real filter’s transmission depends on wavelength; its results do not exactly match this binary RGB representation. The screen material remains white: the displayed colour represents the light reaching its illuminated area.

With these ideal filters, their order does not change the final light, although it does change the light between them. Repeating the same filter does not change the transmitted components either; real filters may further reduce the intensity.
