What we see depends on the light reaching our eyes. Explore your ideas with the apple test.

Before you start

Each diagram shows an apple illuminated by a powerful white spotlight and a dim light bulb. For simplicity, consider white light to consist only of the three primary colours: red, green and blue.

Each apple is shown in the colour seen when both lights are on. What happens when the spotlight is switched off and only the bulb remains on?

DiCoSTEM-TT

Web adaptation of the Grupo Orión test for identifying preconceptions about colour (University of Extremadura). The nine questions, options and original scoring rules are retained, with newly drawn graphics.

Answers are processed on this device and are neither sent nor saved.

About the test and its scientific reference

The original article describes the instrument and four patterns of preconceptions about colour perception.

The article explains why options such as “grey” or “dark grey” were avoided: in pilot tests, respondents tended to use them as an “I don’t know” answer, making their underlying ideas harder to identify. A simple set of colour options is therefore retained.

The test asks about the colour seen under a particular illumination. It uses an idealised model of primary-coloured lights and objects; in real situations, ambient light and colour constancy may also play a part.

PRE1, PRE2 and PRE3 represent mutually exclusive explanations. PRE4 can coexist with any of them. Interpretation therefore considers the response sequence as well as the number of correct answers.

Naranjo Correa, F. L., Martinez Borreguero, G., Perez Rodriguez, A. L., Suero Lopez, M. I., & Pardo Fernandez, P. J. (2016). A new online tool to detect color misconceptions. Color Research & Application, 41(3), 325–329.
